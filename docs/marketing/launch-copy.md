### The problem

**Most unpaid work starts with a friendly "let's just get going".**

- About 20 days a year: the time freelancers spend chasing payments. (IPSE)
- 43% of freelancers have done work they were never paid for. (IPSE)
- 54% of invoices are paid on time. The rest aren't. (FreeAgent)

DirectWork doesn't hold or move money. It makes the terms clear before work
starts and makes payment behaviour visible after it.

### How it works

1. **An owner posts a listing.** Paid work, or a co-founder or partner role. A
   person reviews it before it goes live. Partner listings have to say what's
   already built, the weekly hours, the first milestone and the proposed terms.
2. **You apply in a few minutes.** A short note, up to 3 work samples, and
   answers to up to 3 questions from the owner.
3. **You both accept.** Only then are contact details shared, along with a short
   safety card on what to agree before you start.
4. **You agree a Deal Sheet.** Scope, pay, payment terms, milestones and who
   owns the work. Both of you confirm the same version. (At launch.)
5. **You do the work, then confirm how it went.** Both sides confirm the outcome
   and write references. That becomes a track record confirmed by both sides.

### The Deal Sheet

**One page. Both sides confirm it. Nobody has to remember what was said on a
call.**

After an application is accepted, either side can propose a Deal Sheet. It
covers:

- Scope of the work
- Pay: fixed, hourly, daily, equity-only or mixed
- Payment terms: days after invoice or milestone (14 by default)
- Up to 12 milestones, each with a date and amount
- Who owns the work (IP)
- For partner deals: equity, vesting, cliff and decision rights

Any edit creates a new version, and both of you confirm again. Either of you can
save a dated PDF.

After each payment is due, the professional records whether it was paid on time,
paid late or not paid yet. Owners build a record such as **"Paid on time on 4 of
5 reported payments"**, shown once there are 3 or more undisputed reports. Owners
can dispute a report, and disputed reports don't count while they're reviewed.

For business-to-business deals there's a prefilled late-payment letter citing
the Late Payment of Commercial Debts (Interest) Act 1998, with statutory interest
and fixed compensation worked out. It's a template, not legal advice.

The Deal Sheet is a record of what you agreed. DirectWork doesn't draft it and
isn't a party to it.

### FAQ

**Is DirectWork a social network?**
No. There's no feed, no posts, no likes and no followers. It's for finding work
and finding people.

**Who pays?**
Professionals never pay. Owners can post one live listing free, or use Pro for
more listings and Featured placement.

**Does DirectWork handle payments?**
No. You pay each other directly. DirectWork helps you agree the terms and records
how payment went.

**Is the Deal Sheet a legal contract?**
It's a written record of terms you both confirmed. DirectWork doesn't draft it
and isn't a party to it. For anything significant, especially equity, take your
own legal advice.

**How does the pays-on-time record work?**
After each payment due date, the professional reports whether it was paid on
time, late, or not yet. The owner's record shows how many reported payments were
on time, once there are 3 or more undisputed reports. Owners can dispute any
report within 14 days.

**What stops false reports?**
Reports can only be made on a Deal Sheet both sides confirmed, after the due
date, from accounts that weren't brand new. Disputed reports don't count until
we've reviewed them. Reporting falsely breaks our terms.

**What's a partner listing?**
A co-founder or partner role, usually for equity. It must say what already
exists, the weekly hours, the first milestone, the proposed terms and what the
owner brings beyond the idea.

**Why [CITY] first?**
Reviewing listings properly takes a person who knows the local market. We'd
rather start in one place and do it well.

**When does it open?**
[LAUNCH DATE]. Waitlist members get in first.
