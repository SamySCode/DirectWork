The problem

Most unpaid work starts with a friendly “let’s just get going”.

  • ~20 days a year spent by freelancers chasing payments. Source: IPSE
  • 43% of freelancers have done work they were never paid for. Source: IPSE
  • 54% of invoices are paid on time. The rest aren’t. Source: FreeAgent

DirectWork doesn’t hold or move money. It makes the terms clear before work starts, and makes payment behaviour visible after it.

How it works

Five steps, all of them written down.

  1. An owner posts a listing

    Paid work, or a co-founder or partner role. A person reviews it before it goes live. Partner listings must say what’s already built, the weekly hours, the first milestone and the proposed terms.

  2. You apply in a few minutes

    A short note, up to 3 work samples, and answers to up to 3 questions from the owner.

  3. You both accept

    Only then are contact details shared, with a short safety card on what to agree before you start.

  4. You agree a Deal Sheet At launch

    Scope, pay, payment terms, milestones and who owns the work. You both confirm the same version.

  5. You do the work, then confirm how it went

    Both sides confirm the outcome and write references. That becomes a track record confirmed by both sides.

The Deal Sheet At launch

One page. Both sides confirm it.

Nobody has to remember what was said on a call. After an application is accepted, either side can propose a Deal Sheet.

  • Scope of the work
  • Pay: fixed, hourly, daily, equity-only or mixed
  • Payment terms: days after invoice or milestone, 14 by default
  • Milestones: up to 12, each with a date and amount
  • IP: who owns the work
  • Partner deals: equity, vesting, cliff and decision rights

Any edit creates a new version, and both of you confirm again. Either of you can save a dated PDF. The Deal Sheet is a record of what you agreed: DirectWork doesn’t draft it and isn’t a party to it.

Pays-on-time record, example

Paid on time on 4 of 5 reported payments

After each payment is due, the professional records whether it was paid on time, paid late or not paid yet. The record appears on an owner’s profile once there are 3 or more undisputed reports.

Owners can dispute a report within 14 days. Disputed reports don’t count while we review them. We show counts, never scores or labels.

Paid late on a business deal?

For business-to-business deals there’s a prefilled letter citing the Late Payment of Commercial Debts (Interest) Act 1998, with statutory interest and fixed compensation worked out. It’s a template, not legal advice.

Questions

Frequently asked

Is DirectWork a social network?

No. There’s no feed, no posts, no likes and no followers. It’s for finding work and finding people.

Who pays?

Professionals never pay. Owners can post one live listing free, or use Pro for more listings and Featured placement.

Does DirectWork handle payments?

No. You pay each other directly. DirectWork helps you agree the terms and records how payment went.

Is the Deal Sheet a legal contract?

It’s a written record of terms you both confirmed. DirectWork doesn’t draft it and isn’t a party to it. For anything significant, especially equity, take your own legal advice.

How does the pays-on-time record work?

After each payment due date, the professional reports whether it was paid on time, late, or not yet. The owner’s record shows how many reported payments were on time, once there are 3 or more undisputed reports. Owners can dispute any report within 14 days.

What stops false reports?

Reports can only be made on a Deal Sheet both sides confirmed, after the due date, from accounts that weren’t brand new. Disputed reports don’t count until we’ve reviewed them. Reporting falsely breaks our terms.

What’s a partner listing?

A co-founder or partner role, usually for equity. It must say what already exists, the weekly hours, the first milestone, the proposed terms and what the owner brings beyond the idea.

Why [CITY] first?

Reviewing listings properly takes a person who knows the local market. We’d rather start in one place and do it well.
