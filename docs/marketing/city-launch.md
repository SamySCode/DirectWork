# DirectWork: first launch city

Prepared 2026-11-07. This is desk research only: no interviews and no paid
databases (Dealroom and Beauhurst city pages are gated). Every figure is
dated. Where a figure is old, comes from a vendor, or is my own estimate,
it says so.

**Recommendation: Leeds. Runner-up: Manchester.** The scores are close.
Manchester is the bigger market on almost every size measure. Leeds wins
because a cold-start curated marketplace needs a market it can dominate,
and in Leeds that is cheaper and faster to do. If the founding team lives in
Manchester and not Leeds, choose Manchester instead (see "Tie-breakers").

---

## 1. Method and weights

Each city gets a score from 1 to 5 on each criterion. The weights reflect
what DirectWork has to do before going public: get **100+ curated
professionals and 20+ qualifying listings** in one niche (product,
engineering, design, growth). It needs enough supply, but more importantly
it needs to become _the_ local name quickly and cheaply. Raw market size
helps less than you'd expect at this target, because every city here has
far more than 100 suitable professionals. Trust and attention are the
scarce resources.

| Criterion                                         | Weight | Why this weight                                                                                                                    |
| ------------------------------------------------- | ------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Digital/tech and startup scene size               | 20%    | This is the owner (demand) side. We need startups and small businesses with paid work or credible partner roles.                   |
| Freelancer and self-employed density in the niche | 20%    | This is the professional (supply) side. `PRODUCT_STRATEGY.md` says to recruit professionals first.                                 |
| Universities and graduate pipeline                | 10%    | Useful long-term supply. But owners want experienced people, and fresh graduates are a weaker fit for paid work and partner roles. |
| Launch partners (coworking, meetups, events)      | 20%    | Partners are how a curated, offline-first launch reaches people without paid ads.                                                  |
| Cost of reaching people                           | 20%    | Covers competition for attention, media fragmentation, and ad and event costs. This decides cost per accepted member.              |
| Regional support ecosystem                        | 10%    | Accelerator and growth-hub cohorts are ready-made owner pipelines. It helps, but it isn't decisive.                                |

**Geography.** Statistics are for the core local authority (LA) unless
marked as combined authority (CA). This matters for Manchester: Manchester
LA is tightly drawn and leaves out Salford (MediaCity) and Trafford, so
Greater Manchester (GM) figures are shown as well.

How to read these figures:

- APS figures for a single local authority come from small samples. The
  confidence intervals (±) overlap, so treat the self-employment ranking as
  suggestive, not proven.
- Self-employment covers **every sector**, not just tech.
- Small SIC 62 companies (0–4 employees) are a better proxy for tech
  freelancers and contractors. Many of them are one-person limited
  companies. They still undercount sole traders and designers or marketers
  who work outside SIC 62.
- SIC 73 (advertising and market research) is a rough proxy for growth and
  marketing freelancers. Firm counts: Leeds 365, Manchester 420, Birmingham
  300, Bristol 235, Edinburgh 180.

**Sensitivity check.** With size-heavy weights (scene 35%, freelancers 20%,
universities 10%, partners 15%, cost 10%, support 10%), the result flips:
Manchester 4.58, Leeds 4.30. The choice depends on whether you value _size_
or _winnability_. For a first city that must reach liquidity by hand,
winnability is the right priority. Size matters more for city two.

### Why each city scored as it did

- **Leeds.**
  - It has the most software firms, the most micro software firms and the
    highest self-employment rate of the five core local authorities.
  - The Data City counts it as the largest tech cluster by company number
    outside London.
  - Leeds Digital Festival is an open, community-run platform: 220 events
    and 198 organising companies in 2026. Its Mini-Fest on 20–22 April 2027
    falls in our window. There is a tech hub next to the station (Platform)
    and a university innovation hub (Nexus).
  - Tech West Yorkshire is brand new (June 2026), backed by the combined
    authority, and wants to connect existing groups. It is a natural first
    partner.
  - The media market is smaller (Yorkshire Post, TheBusinessDesk Yorkshire,
    BusinessCloud, Prolific North, Bdaily), so one good story reaches a
    larger share of the community.
  - It lost points on scene size: venture funding is lower than in
    Manchester, and the recent figures are weak.
- **Manchester.**
  - It is the largest northern ecosystem. Greater Manchester has 4,390 SIC
    62 firms against West Yorkshire's 3,065, it has more venture funding,
    and it has the biggest university base.
  - In-window events are strong: Manchester Digital Festival on 8–12 Feb
    2027, and likely Manchester Tech Week and DTX in late April 2027.
  - But it is the most contested market for attention: recruiters,
    agencies, many event series, and Manchester Digital's own talent fair
    and job board. The partner landscape is rich but fragmented and often
    pay-to-play, which pushes up cost per member.
- **Bristol.**
  - It has the best recent funding data (South West £1.47bn in 2025) and
    dense micro software firms per head.
  - techSPARK is one very efficient channel. It also runs a mature
    community job board, which is partly a competitor.
  - The Bristol local authority itself has a low self-employment rate
    (9.3%).
  - It is far from the other target cities, so it is a weaker base for
    expanding to a second city.
- **Birmingham.**
  - It has a large university base and growing venture funding (KPMG says
    Midlands VC rose in Q2 2025).
  - But niche firms are sparse per head (13 per 10k adults), the startup
    community is less dense and more spread out, and the October 2025
    report documents funding gaps.
- **Edinburgh.**
  - It has the highest share of graduates (70.6% at RQF4+) and strong
    institutions (CodeBase, Techscaler, Scottish Enterprise).
  - But the self-employment rate is lower. Media, support schemes and the
    legal context are separate from England, so a launch there doesn't
    spill over to the next city. It is the best "other" city, ahead of
    Newcastle and Sheffield.
  - Newcastle was considered and rejected: it has 405 SIC 62 firms, a third
    of Leeds's count.

### Tie-breakers (decide before committing)

1. **Founder presence beats any half-point here.** A curated launch needs
   the operator at two or three local events a week. If the operator lives
   near Manchester and not Leeds, choose Manchester.
2. **Leeds doesn't rule out Manchester.** The two cities are about 50
   minutes apart by train. A Leeds launch can take in Manchester-based
   professionals and become the "Northern" launch by month 6. Starting in
   Bristol or Edinburgh would not give that.

## 4. Leeds 90-day launch plan (9 Nov 2026 – 7 Feb 2027)

This follows `PRODUCT_STRATEGY.md` Phase 1: recruit professionals first,
accept only listings that meet the admission criteria, and make
introductions by hand (double opt-in). One named operator owns the plan.

**Gate before going public:** 100+ accepted professionals **and** 20+
approved listings, plus at least 10 hand-made introductions that led to a
real conversation. The public launch is planned for the **Leeds Digital
Mini-Fest on 20–22 April 2027**. If the gates are met early, do a soft
public launch in February and use the Mini-Fest as the "first results"
moment.

- **Professionals:** about 220 waitlist sign-ups, then about 150 complete
  applications, then about 100 accepted (around a two-thirds acceptance
  rate). Target mix: about 40 engineering, 25 product and design, 20 growth
  and marketing, 15 data and other.
- **Owners:** about 60 conversations, then about 35 draft listings, then
  20+ approved. Aim for about 14 paid work and about 6 partner (co-founder)
  listings, all meeting the Phase 0 admission criteria.
- Paid-work listings are easier to qualify and build trust faster, so they
  should be the majority.
